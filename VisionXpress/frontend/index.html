THE PLANNING CHALLENGE

Complexities in Railway Maintenance Block Scheduling

Railway track maintenance requires reconciling multiple engineering demands against strict operational and passenger safety constraints.

01

Multiple Maintenance Sources

Permanent Way (Civil), Traction Distribution (Electrical), and Signalling & Telecom units generate maintenance requirements independently using disparate departmental logging systems.

02

Varying Defect Priorities

Requests vary from routine preventive patrols to severe track geometry defects and emergency catenary adjustments, requiring an objective, transparent basis for prioritization.

03

Limited Operational Windows

High-density railway corridors operate near capacity, leaving narrow intervals between train movements where maintenance blocks can be feasibly granted without inducing passenger delays.

04

Inter-Departmental Conflicts

Without coordinated scheduling, maintenance blocks risk overlapping in incompatible spatial zones, causing mutual interference or underutilization of precious track possession time.

THE APPROACH

How VisionXpress Structures Maintenance Planning

A systematic decision-support pipeline designed to transition manual ad-hoc requests into validated, coordinated corridor candidate plans.

Step 1

Ingest Requirements

Collect structured maintenance requests across departments with spatial locations, defect severity ratings, machinery requirements, and required durations.

Step 2

Prioritize Work

Calculate multi-factor priority scores considering defect severity, failure risk, safety criticality, asset status, and scheduled target dates.

Step 3

Identify Feasible Slots

Evaluate scheduled train movements and existing block sanctions to discover feasible time intervals where work can be safely executed.

Step 4

Coordinate Compatible Tasks

Group compatible tasks from different engineering branches working on the same corridor section into common coordinated blocks.

Step 5

Facilitate BDMS Review

Provide the Block Decision Management System desk with candidate plans, alternative slot comparisons, and clear sanction controls.

Step 6

Track Decisions & Status

Maintain full lifecycle tracking from initial submission to final sanction or rejection, supported by in-app notifications.

PROTOTYPE EVALUATION SCOPE

What the Current Prototype Demonstrates

An honest overview of what is implemented and testable in the current software release.

Currently Implemented & Demonstrable

  • 5 Department Roles: Switchable access for TMS (Track), TDMS (Traction), SMMS (Signals), COA (Timetable), and BDMS (Controller).
  • Automated Plan Generation: Generates candidate block allocations from pending maintenance requests via backend optimization logic.
  • Interactive Block Modification: Modal interface allowing operators to explore alternative time slots and recalculate feasibility.
  • Bulk Template Ingestion: Ingestion and validation of Excel (.xlsx) files for maintenance tasks and COA train schedules.
  • Pre-Loaded Demo Scenario: One-click demo dataset generation (`/api/demo/load`) for instant evaluator walkthrough.
  • Persistent SQLite Database: Relational storage tracking requests, blocks, decisions, and system alerts.

Intended Role & Honest Boundaries

  • Decision-Support Tool: Provides recommendations and conflict checks; does not replace final human controller authority.
  • Algorithmic Nature: Utilizes deterministic optimization heuristics and multi-criteria scoring; does not require opaque deep learning.
  • Simulated Data Environment: Tested against representative divisional corridor schedules and synthetic maintenance sets.
  • Hackathon Scope: Demonstrates operational concept feasibility; not yet interfaced with live railway production feeds.

DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Designed for Operational Decision Support

Guiding principles ensuring the software remains practical, transparent, and aligned with railway operational reality.

Human-in-the-Loop Authority

The system is explicitly designed as decision support. Section Controllers maintain final sanction authority and can inspect or modify any proposed maintenance window.

Transparent Deterministic Rules

Prioritization and slot allocation follow clear, explainable engineering rules rather than opaque models, ensuring controllers can understand why a slot was suggested.

Cross-Functional Visibility

Breaks down inter-departmental silos by giving civil, traction, and signalling engineers common situational awareness of corridor block availability.
